Running pace over time

four ways to look at pace vs. effort

Every run here has its first 5 minutes excluded (heart rate lags actual effort at the start) and likely sensor dropouts or stale readings across data gaps filtered out; runs without enough clean data are left out entirely rather than shown as an unreliable average.

Efficiency Factor

Speed ÷ average heart rate per run (×100) — a standard way to trend aerobic fitness that isolates how efficiently you're running at whatever effort a given run happened to be, instead of comparing pace only within whichever zone that effort landed in. Higher is more efficient.

Predicted pace at 150 bpm

Each run's own pace-vs-heart-rate relationship, read off at a fixed reference heart rate instead of whichever zone a run happened to spend time in. Lower is faster at the same effort. Only shown for runs where heart rate varied enough within the run to fit a trend.

Efficiency Factor — easy runs only

The same Efficiency Factor as above, limited to steady/easy runs (almost no time above Zone 3) so interval workouts don't mix hard and easy effort into one trend.

Pace by heart-rate zone

Average pace at each HR zone, by period — every 15-second sample from every run is tagged by the zone your heart rate was actually in at that moment, so a run's warmup/tempo/cooldown segments each count toward their own zone instead of averaging into one misleading number. A period/zone combination with under 5 minutes of data is left out of the trend rather than shown as an unreliable average.

Same-route comparison

Runs that start in the same place and cover the same distance are almost certainly the same physical loop, so pace differences here reflect fitness rather than a harder or hillier course. Only routes run at least 7 times are shown; each dot is one run.
